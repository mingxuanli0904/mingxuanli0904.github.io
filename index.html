About

I am a final-year PhD researcher in Area Studies at the University of Oxford, UK. My research interests include reproductive health, the dynamics of care, and the social life of therapeutics and illness. My doctoral research explores pregnancy-related health care in China, with a specific focus on how care is delivered through the collaboration of medical institutions, families, and individuals. Building on this collaborative model, I am also extending my research to chronic disease and varied healthcare contexts.

My thesis, Caring, Managing, and Controlling: Prenatal Care across Hospital, Family, and Self in Urban China, draws on thirteen months of ethnographic fieldwork with 51 pregnant women and new mothers.

Before Oxford, I studied anthropology at Xiamen University and the Chinese University of Hong Kong, and completed an MA in Oral History at Columbia University.

Research

Prenatal care across hospital, family, and self

My doctoral project follows pregnant women as they move between hospital appointments, family care, and their own routines of monitoring and self-care. It asks who takes responsibility for a pregnancy, and how that responsibility is shared, delegated, or imposed.

Care, authority, and intergenerational relations

I examine how authority over a pregnancy is negotiated within families, through the idea of guan and through changing relations between generations.

Digital health and AI in pregnancy

I also study how women use AI tools and social media platforms such as RedNote to seek information and manage symptoms during pregnancy, and what these tools change in relations between women, their families, and doctors.

Teaching and mentoring

Alongside my doctoral research, I design and supervise independent research projects for advanced secondary school students. Recent projects have covered AI ethics, gender and climate change using qualitative methods, gaps between supply and demand in smart eldercare in China, and AI and equity in education.
